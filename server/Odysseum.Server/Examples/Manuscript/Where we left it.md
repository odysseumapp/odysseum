She drew the island one more time, at the kitchen table in the white house, with the windows open and the sea doing what it always did.

The spine. The crooked shoulder. The harbor that faced the wrong way.

The road, up from the jetty, past the tower that was back where she remembered it, to the door of the house she was sitting in. She drew the lighthouse with three windows and a crooked door, and did not draw the fourth window that Elin had bricked up the winter after the photograph was taken.

When she came to the northern shore she stopped.

She knew it now. She had walked it. She could have drawn every cove.

She left it blank.

Elin came in from the garden and looked over her shoulder and said nothing, which was the kindest thing anyone had ever said to her.

---

Tomas was waiting at the jetty with the engine running and his collar turned up against a wind that had not yet arrived.

“You’ll be back,” he said. It was not a question. It was not quite a statement either.

“Probably.”

“That’s the right answer.”

Halfway across the bay she went out on deck and unfolded the little map, the one in her own childhood handwriting. The road was exactly as long as it should be. The coastline held still under her eyes.

Behind her, the bell rang once.

She did not turn around. She had decided what she was looking for, and she knew where she had left it.
