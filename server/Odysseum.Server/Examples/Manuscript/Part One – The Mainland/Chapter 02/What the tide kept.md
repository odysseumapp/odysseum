Halfway across the bay, the rain stopped.

It did not fade. One moment it was there, tapping a thousand small fingers against the cabin roof, and the next it was gone. Ahead of them, the water lay perfectly still.

Mara went out on deck.

The mainland had disappeared. In its place was a low bank of cloud, gray as the inside of a shell. Tomas stood at the wheel with his collar turned up and his eyes fixed on something she could not see.

“Is it always like this?”

“Only when you’re coming back.”

On the starboard side, a church bell rang.

Mara turned. The bell tower stood on a spit of land so narrow that the sea appeared to pass beneath it. She knew that tower. She had climbed its stairs on a dare when she was ten.

But it had always been on the other side of the island.

She unfolded the little map.

The coastline was different again.

Tomas glanced at the paper, then at her face.

“Best put that away until we get there.”

“Why?”

“Because you haven’t decided what you’re looking for yet.”

Behind them, the bell rang once more. This time it sounded much farther away.
