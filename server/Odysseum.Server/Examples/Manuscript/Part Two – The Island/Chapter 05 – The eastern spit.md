The eastern spit was exactly as long as she remembered, which should have been the first warning.

She paced it. Three hundred and twelve steps from the last house to the point, the same number she had counted at ten, when a hundred of her steps had been a good deal shorter. The island had kept the distance and let the measure change. It was, she thought, a very cartographer’s kind of lie.

Where the tower had stood there was a square of paler grass and, in the middle of it, a stair.

Stone steps. Worn in the centres. Going down.

She counted them, of course. Sixty-one, and then water.

---

“You found it,” Elin said, from the path.

She had brought a flask, because she was Elin, and two cups, because she had known.

“The tower moved. The stairs didn’t.”

“Stairs are stubborn. You climbed them on purpose; nobody climbs a tower by accident.” Elin poured. “The island keeps what was meant.”

Mara looked at the water lapping at the sixty-second step.

“Then where does it put the rest?”

Elin handed her a cup and did not answer, and for once Mara understood that this was not evasion. It was that Elin had been standing on this spit, alone, every week for forty years, and had never once been able to see the bottom.
