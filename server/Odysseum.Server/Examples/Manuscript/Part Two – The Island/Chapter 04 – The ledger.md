The shop opened at seven and Nan Hollis had already been up for three hours, which she mentioned twice.

“Surveyor’s girl,” she said, before Mara had reached the counter. “Tea, brown bread, and you’ll want a pencil. Yours is too short.”

“How do you—”

“Everybody’s is, who came from that house.” She set the tea down. “You’ve seen the tower, then.”

“It’s on the wrong side.”

“It’s on *a* side. That’s more than some of them.”

---

The ledger lived under the counter, wrapped in a tea towel. Nan did not let Mara hold it. She turned the pages herself, licking her thumb, until she came to a spread headed *EAST*.

Places, in a column. Beside each one, a name, and beside most of the names a neat pencil line.

*The eastern spit — M. Vale.*

No line.

“That’s you,” Nan said. “Last person to mention it. Twelve years ago, on the jetty, telling your mother you’d climbed the tower on a dare. I wrote it down that evening.”

“So it’s still there.”

“The spit is. The tower had other ideas.” Nan closed the book. “You remembered the stairs, see. Sixty-one. You didn’t remember where they were.”

---

Outside, Father Quill was standing under nothing at all, on the harbor wall, looking up.

“Sixty,” he said, as Mara passed. “Or sixty-one. I lose count near the top.”

“Count of what?”

He looked at her as though she had asked what the sea was for.

“Rings, girl. Somebody has to.”
