Tomas told her on the jetty, in the end, with his back to the island, because he could not have said it facing her.

“He asked me to keep bringing the boat.”

“That’s all?”

“That’s a great deal, from him.” He coiled a rope that did not need coiling. “Every crossing. Whether the timetable said so or not. Whether anybody was waiting. *Somebody will need it,* he said, *and she won’t believe the timetable either.*”

Mara looked at the little blue boat rising and falling against its ropes.

“She.”

“He never said the island’s name. Nor yours.” Tomas shrugged. “I stopped needing to ask which.”

---

That night the bell rang once, from the east, and Father Quill counted it out loud from his bed and was, for the first time in his life, certain.

In the lighthouse Elin stood a long while at the place where the fourth window had been, with her steady hands flat on the brick, and then went down to the kitchen and did not put the kettle on, and did not go out on her rounds.

Some places, she had decided, could be allowed to go.

---

In the morning the tower stood on the eastern spit with sixty-one dry steps inside it, and nobody on Bellwether, including the woman who had put it there, had seen it move.
