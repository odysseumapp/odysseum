She drew the first cove at dusk, sitting on a rock with the map on her knee, and by morning the cove was a straight line of shingle and the map agreed with it.

She drew the second at noon, watching it the whole time. It lasted until she blinked.

On the third day she walked the northern shore without the pencil, only looking, and the coastline stayed exactly, ridiculously, as it was: a coast that had never been surveyed, because every survey had been a claim, and the island did not permit claims here.

---

“You’re not drawing,” Elin said that evening.

“No.”

“Good.”

Mara turned the little map over. On the back, in her father’s hand this time, so faint she had never seen it:

> *Leave the north.*

Not an instruction. A description. He had left the north blank, every time, and she had thought it was because he had never walked it.

He had walked it every week.
