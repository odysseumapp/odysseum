He does not sleep so much as wait, these days, and so he is already awake when it starts.

One.

He is out of bed by the second, which does not come. He stands in the doorway of his room at the foot of a tower that is, tonight, on the west side of the island, and he waits with the door open and the cold coming in.

The sea says nothing useful.

After a while he understands that there will be no second ring, and that this is the first time in fifty years the count has stopped at one.

“Well,” he says to the dark. “Somebody remembered something.”

He goes back to bed. He does not, for once, disagree with the count.
