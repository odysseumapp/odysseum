The timetable said there had never been a ferry to Bellwether.

Mara read it twice.

Around her, the harbor had the unmistakable look of a place that had been waiting a very long time. Paint flaked from the ticket office. A gull stood on a bollard with an air of permanent disappointment. The sea, at least, was where she had left it.

“You’re late.”

The man was sitting on an overturned crate, repairing a net that appeared to belong to no boat in particular. His hands moved without hesitation. His eyes did not leave hers.

“The timetable—”

“Doesn’t say what you want it to say. They rarely do.”

She looked past him. At the end of the jetty, a small blue boat rose and fell against its ropes. On its side, barely visible beneath three coats of paint, was the name she remembered.

*The Patience.*

“My father used to take that boat.”

The man’s hands stopped.

For a moment the only sound was the water knocking against the wooden steps.

“What did you say your name was?”

“I didn’t.”

“No,” he said, gathering the net. “I suppose you didn’t.”

He stood and walked toward the boat. Mara followed, carrying one suitcase and an atlas that felt heavier with every step.
